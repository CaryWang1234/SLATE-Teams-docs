Office · working like a person

If a duty roster were only "wake the model when the clock says so", it would be a cron job. This layer exists so an employee remembers the last run, orders their own queue, says why something didn't happen, and can hand work to a colleague.

#He remembers the last run

Every employee keeps their own notes, written with "Leave yourself a note", readable only by that employee at the start of their next run. Three kinds:

  • open — something not finished yet.
  • ledger — the conclusion of a finished thing.
  • blocked — what condition is missing before it can move.

At the start of a run those three piles appear as 未闭环 (open) / 卡住 (stuck) / 已定论 (settled), most recent first.

Cross it out when it's done

When a thing is genuinely finished, write a ledger note and cross out the matching open note. Leave it and the next run does the same thing again — not a bug, but the direct consequence of "memory only holds what he wrote down".

#Morning triage

Triage orders the jobs under his own name, one batch at a time:

ActionMeaning
keepWorth doing. Priority 0–9, higher gets claimed first; optionally with a deadline
deferHold it, look again tomorrow
dropJudged not worth doing — a reason is mandatory

Triage can only touch his own queue, never anyone else's.

#"Not worth doing" needs your nod

An employee may judge a job pointless, but that judgement does not take effect: after a drop the job parks under "Waiting on you", where you get two buttons:

  • Agree it should not be done, close it out — accept the judgement, the job is settled.
  • Not right — put it back — overrule it, the job returns to the queue.

Same shape when a run can't continue: "Close out this run" has exactly two statuses, each with its own door for you:

  • Blocked — the missing condition is written on the line; supply it and press "补齐条件，放回队列" (fill in the condition, put it back).
  • Judged not worth doing — the reason is on the line; it only settles when you nod.

Both wait for a human. No job ever disappears quietly.

#Hand-offs between desks

"Message a Colleague" turns one sentence into a new job scheduled under the other employee — you don't wait for a reply. The recipient is one of three things:

  • a colleague's name or employee id;
  • user — said to you only, it lands in your notifications;
  • broadcast — pinned to the office feed where everyone sees it.

One message, one thing to do: say what is wanted, don't paste the process.

hop {n}

Hand-offs are counted: the feed shows 第 1 手 / 第 2 手 ("hop 1", "hop 2"…), counting round trips between colleagues. At hop 6 nothing gets woken automatically — the message is still left there. That rule exists so two colleagues can't kick a ball to each other forever. Deadline reminders the clock raises don't count as a hop: a clock has no round trip.
