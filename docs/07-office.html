Office · hiring, rosters, deliveries

The office is collaboration at different moments: you leave a piece of work, an AI staff member does it on their shift, you check it when you are back. It does not add a background service — the open SLATE Teams page is what executes.

Close the app and nothing runs

The backend only queues; it never executes work itself. So the office needs one machine with SLATE Teams open: that page is whoever is on duty. With the app closed, jobs sit quietly in the queue until a page is back.

#What the panel looks like

Roster on the left with a floor plan of the desks (who's busy, who's free, who is handing something over), a message box in the middle that can "交给" (assign to) one employee, "贴出来" (post for everyone) or "交办" (hand over a job), and five tabs on the right: 动态 (feed), 等你拍板 (waiting on you), 值班表 (duties), 岗位 (roles), 站会与绩效 (standup & record). This chapter covers roles and duties; the last two live in Working like a person and Gates & accounts.

#Hiring

Under Hire there are two ways in: "Hire from a template" (four built in: researcher, engineer, reviewer, duty keeper), or fill in a new colleague from scratch. Each employee carries these fields:

FieldWhat it decides
Name / avatar / hueColleagues address each other by name — names must be unique
One line describing the role / 目标 (goal) / DutiesWhat they do and what they aim at; also what triage weighs when deciding if a job is theirs
Model / rounds / projectWhich model, how many turns one run may take, which project root the work lands in
the reviewerWhether a delivery passes a human and who reviews it; "No reviewer (deliveries come straight to you)" ships it to you
Daily budgetHow many tokens per day before the breaker trips (see Gates & accounts)
UnattendedWhich approval level their shifts run at

"解雇" (fire) takes them off the roster — the jobs they did and the things they said stay in the account, and their record row is marked 已离职 (left).

#Scheduling

"Add duty" puts a shift on an employee. Two kinds of trigger:

  • Daily / every N minutes / once — the clock comes to look.
  • Event Trigger — a commit landed, watched paths changed, a self-check script went red, a Git push, or an external callback.

The clock firing is not the same as "act now". Each shift can carry a Guard, most often:

  • "No more than a few tasks pending" — set to 0 and it means only move when my hands are empty.
  • "Only schedule inside this window".

When a guard can't be read, or a condition type is no longer understood, the panel shows that as a line of text instead of silently skipping.

Why not fire on schedule alone

Firing on time lets one employee pick up another job before finishing the last, and the queue just grows. A guard turns "should I move" into a check with a readable answer.

#The delivery queue

Each employee on the roster shows one state — Idle, Working, Off duty — followed by counters: pending, waiting on you, awaiting review, returned, budget spent. Job states are few enough to just learn:

StateMeaning
Queued · {when}Scheduled, waiting for an open page to claim it
WorkingA page holds it under a lease; if that page dies, the job is claimable again
{n} awaiting reviewDone, waiting for the reviewer
Waiting on youListed under "Waiting on you" — it needs your call
BlockedStuck, and the line says what it is blocked on
Settled / {n} failedFinished / broke, with the reason on that line

Every "didn't happen" has to produce a line you can read. There is no silent retry and no silent discard.
