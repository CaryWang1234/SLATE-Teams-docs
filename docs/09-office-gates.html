Office · gates & accounts

Handing work to AI staff needs a few gates: deliveries shouldn't land on you unexamined, spend has to have a ceiling, and an expired deadline shouldn't rot on the desk. All four live in this layer, and every number is computed from the same jobs table — there is no second ledger.

#The review gate

When you hire someone you pick "the reviewer" for them. Their deliveries go to that reviewer first and not straight to you.

  • Review is itself a job scheduled under the reviewer: it shouldn't sit behind ordinary duty work, so it queues slightly ahead — but anything triage raised above it still goes first. Its deadline defaults to two hours.
  • The reviewer closes it with "Judge This Delivery", and there are exactly two verdicts: approve (it passes, and this is the first time the delivery reaches you) or return (it fails, back to the author, with what to change written down).
  • The reviewer must exist in the roster, cannot review themselves, and cannot review for someone who has left the roster.
The wording you see differs

Before review passes you only get "An employee sent a delivery for review"; after it passes you get "Employee delivery". When it comes back the line reads "Returned in review: why". That split is deliberate — something that hasn't passed shouldn't look like a delivery.

A returned job is re-done through the same hand-off channel, so it counts in "hop {n}" too and stops at hop 6.

#Daily budget

Each employee can carry a Daily budget in tokens. Once today's spend reaches it:

  • the duty roster schedules no new work, and the job shows up as Blocked with the reason on the line;
  • morning triage and your own "Run now" still schedule. Otherwise a full budget would lock you out of even asking them to finish the last item in hand.

The record column reads "{used today} / cap {budget}" and marks Budget spent once it's over.

#Expired deadlines escalate to a human

If triage gave a job a deadline, the clock speaks for the human once it passes: a system line appears in the feed and the job moves into "Waiting on you". The same reminder fires once — refreshing doesn't nag you again — and each pass escalates only a handful of jobs, because flooding the feed is the same as not reminding anyone.

#Two cards

Today's standup — every shift that had "Morning triage" checked leaves one self-reported line: who, what they said, when. If nobody has reported it says so and tells you to put a triage shift on someone, rather than showing you an empty table.

Record — one row per employee: {n} done, {n} failed, {n} waiting on you, {n} awaiting review, {n} returned, how long a run takes on average, and how many tokens went today.

What "average" measures

That column spans claim to close-out, not end-to-end wall clock — time spent waiting in the queue isn't counted. It's an order of magnitude, not a receipt.
